How to use the cluster well

Six habits, each with the number from this page that justifies it. The two at the top are worth more than everything else combined.

1

If your job can checkpoint, submit to a *_requeue partition

The same H100 costs 418 per GPU-hour there against 2649 on kempner_h100. Identical hardware — you are paying for the right not to be interrupted.

6.3× cheaper

2

No cross-GPU communication? Ask for kempner_rtx

Sweeps, one seed per GPU, eval fleets and data generation never touch the interconnect. It is the cheapest dedicated partition and holds more memory per card than the 80 GiB parts. Gradient sync (DDP, FSDP, ZeRO) is the exception — those nodes reach a neighbour at only 53 GB/s.

96 GiB per card

3

Hours you burn now slow everyone's queue later

There is no hard GPU-hour budget here; the cost is fairshare. ydu_lab has drawn 2.5× its slice, so new jobs from the whole account queue behind lighter users.

2.5× our share

4

Check the quota, never df

df reports the whole shared array and says everything is fine while we sit at our own ceiling. Ours: quota -g ydu_lab /n/netscratch — 819 GiB left. Every allocation also has a separate file-count limit.

99% full

5

/n/netscratch is scratch, not storage

Files there are deleted after 90 days and are not backed up. Keep only what can be regenerated or re-downloaded; a directory has vanished from under this lab before.

90-day purge

6

Request the cores you will use, and use the GPU you hold

Job Defense Shield mails the lab when a job takes 4 cores and uses 1, or holds a GPU at 0% utilisation. Both waste fairshare that everyone shares. jobstats <jobid> shows what a job actually used.

If you cannot be preempted, then card choice matters, and kempner_rtx is the value pick among the dedicated partitions: cheapest of them, and 96 GiB per card fits models the 80 GiB parts cannot. The tradeoff is the interconnect — measured here, an RTX card reaches its neighbour at only 53 GB/s, PCIe speed and 27× slower than reaching its own memory. So anything that synchronises every step (DDP, FSDP, ZeRO, tensor parallelism) can lose more to communication than it saves.
Most of what this lab runs does not synchronise at all. A hyperparameter sweep, one seed per GPU, an eval or inference fleet, a data-generation rollout — each GPU minds its own work, nothing crosses the interconnect, and more cards for the same fairshare is simply more throughput.
